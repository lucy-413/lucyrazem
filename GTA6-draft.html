Grand Theft Auto VI

A spoiler-free review by Lucy

Playing Grand Theft Auto V for the first time as a child at my friend's house after he got a new Xbox was a shock, since I was used to playing simple Mario games on my Nintendo Wii. Suddenly, I felt like I could do anything I wanted in a limitless open world. GTA V was my favourite game for part of my teenage years, and I always wondered how Rockstar Games could make something bigger than that. Red Dead Redemption II gave us all a sneak peek of what a future Grand Theft Auto game could look like, but because of its setting, it still didn't answer many of the questions I had. In this review, I'll share my thoughts after playing 30-ish hours of GTA VI.

I am now 20 years old, and despite all my critique of the modern videogame industry, and the horrible anti-consuemer practises that Rockstar Games is doing. The final weeks before GTA VI felt like waiting for Santa to bring me a present as a child.

This review is based on my playtrough of the preordered Ultimate Edition of Grand Theft Auto VI, played on a Playstation 5 Slim.

Visiting Leonida

Despite its age, Grand Theft Auto: Vice City still has my favourite GTA map, so I knew I was going to love GTA VI's Leonida. After finishing the prologue, I started doing some activities in Key Lento, and it immediately felt way more alive than I expected. Small towns like Paleto Bay in GTA V are extremely empty and don't have much to do, so I was afraid GTA VI's towns would suffer the same fate. The game doesn't stop you from exploring the entire map right away, but there is so much to do in Key Lento that I didn't feel the need to go past the Keys and the swamps. Every time I got in my car, I'd run into a random event that could lead to an unexpected side activity, so even when I was planning to do a mission, I'd often find myself taking a detour to help some random NPC out.

How GTA VI feels to play

One thing I liked about RDR2 was that your horse was such a big part of the gameplay - you could steal other people's horses, but it was risky and practically pointless since your own horse carried all your stuff and weapons. I wanted GTA VI to do the same thing, but I thought it was impossible due to the game's faster-paced gameplay. In GTA V, you rarely use your own car since you can just walk into the middle of the street, steal the first one you see with the press of a button, and drive off. You can still do that in GTA VI, but Rockstar managed to balance it so well that in Chapter I, I'd find myself ordering a taxi to drive me to my own car rather than stealing an NPC's vehicle. The police are a lot smarter, though a 1- or 2-star chase is still relatively easy to escape. Your car can carry your weapons and loot, which makes it more than just a vehicle.

Another thing is the game's longer day-night cycle. I actually felt the need to go back to Jason's house when it started getting late, and once I got there, a whole other list of activities was waiting for me. You never visited your houses in GTA V because there was really nothing to do. In GTA VI, you can eat, sleep, wash up, change your clothes, work out, hide money and loot, fish, and even renovate the place, which is a cool mini-activity.
